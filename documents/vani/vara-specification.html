Section 1

Positioning & principles

Vara is a subscription agent in the VaNi family that runs the front half of talent acquisition for a tenant: it composes job definitions, converses with candidates, ingests whatever they share, screens with evidence, ranks by probability, and hands humans a decision that is already prepared — never already made.

Vara lives inside the VaNiGTM codebase, tagged Talent, reusing the GTM funnel spine (intake → enrich → score → route → communicate). Like Nova, it carries its own name, its own commercial gateway, and its own metering. First tenant: Vikuna internal; then externalised.

Principles (each one is testable in the product)

PrincipleWhat it means in the build
Rules reject, models rank, humans decideOnly deterministic knockout rules can close an application without a human. Model scores order candidates; they never terminate anyone. Every decision of record has a human name on it.
Nothing closes instantlyEvery below-threshold candidate enters a closing window (default 3 days, per-JD configurable). The rejection message fires only when the timer expires untouched. Humans can Rescue, Hold, or Close-now at any point inside the window.
Evidence, not adjectivesEvery score component cites its source — a chat answer, a resume line, a public signal. If a number cannot show its evidence, it does not ship.
Learning is gatedVara proposes calibration changes; a named human approves. A threshold can never rise without explicit approval. Every scoring config is versioned and immutable.
No black holesEvery applicant is acknowledged on application and informed on decision — on their channel (email + WhatsApp), within a stated SLA, including rejections.
Consent-clean data onlyCandidate data enters via the candidate’s own hand (upload, link, chat). No scraping. Consent, retention, and deletion are product features, not policy documents.
Attention compresses at volumeAt 250 applications, the human sees the ~9 above the line and Vara’s picks below it. Everything else is reachable (search, ranked lists, bulk actions) but never demanded.
Vikuna is tenant #1, not a special casevikuna.io is a real tenant row — its own tenant_id, onboarding record, embed config and metering — even though it shares the folder structure. Any feature that works for Vikuna only because it is “the home tenant” is a bug. Dogfooding is the multi-tenant test.
Agents extend, never modifyPlatform tables (vani_) never grow agent-specific columns. An agent ships as its own prefixed tables (vara_) hanging off platform keys. Activating an agent adds rows; deactivating removes them; the org’s structure is untouched either way.
Section 2

Personas

P1 · TENANT ADMIN

Head of HR / Ops lead

Owns the subscription. Onboards the organisation. Sets policy.

Configures comms channels, compliance posture, role families and defaults. Approves calibration proposals. Watches metering and loop health.

Cares about: defensibility, cost, brand of the candidate experience.
P2 · TA PROFESSIONAL

Recruiter / Talent acquisition

Daily driver. The human in “humans decide”.

Composes JDs with Vara, watches the probability map, works the closing window (rescue/hold/close), advances candidates, hands over to hiring managers.

Cares about: time-to-shortlist, not missing a good one, defensible closes.
P3 · HIRING MANAGER

The role’s owner

Occasional user. Receives a prepared decision.

Reviews a ranked, evidence-backed shortlist. Verdicts: Interview or Pass-with-reason. Never touches the pipeline mechanics.

Cares about: quality of the top 5, zero admin, his verdict being heard.
P4 · CANDIDATE

Applicant — any domain

Engineer, salesperson, nurse, delivery lead. Applies once, remembered respectfully.

Applies through a conversation, not a form. Told the rules up front (band, notice, knockouts). Gets status honestly, on their channel, whatever the outcome.

Cares about: respect, speed, honesty, control of their data.
P5 · VANI OPERATOR

Vikuna platform team

Runs Vara as a product across tenants.

Provisions tenants, monitors agent health and loop-health circuit breakers across the fleet, manages domain packs, operates the commercial gateway (per-candidate metering, invoicing).

Cares about: tenant isolation, unit economics, one codebase serving every domain.
Section 3

Multi-tenancy & subscription

Vara is multi-tenant by construction. A tenant is an organisation that subscribes to the Vara agent; nothing works for a tenant until onboarding completes.

DecisionSpecification
Tenancy modelSingle deployment, shared schema, row-level security on tenant_id everywhere (consistent with the VaNi base-framework decision: same domain, different config — no per-tenant tables).
Subscription unitThe tenant subscribes to the Vara agent — not to VaNiGTM as a whole. Vara carries its own commercial gateway, consistent with the per-agent pricing model of the VaNi family.
Metering unitPer candidate processed (scored past knockouts). Knockout-only closes meter at a reduced rate. JD creation, comms, and pool storage are inclusive. Metering events are written at score time — the same moment the score snapshot is stored.
Tenant isolationCandidates, JDs, scores, comms, calibration history: all tenant-scoped. The talent pool is per tenant — candidates are never shared or matched across tenants. Domain packs and the platform code are the only shared artifacts.
LLM providerBring-your-own per tenant (Org OS thesis): tenant connects their provider credentials during onboarding; extraction and conversation run against the tenant’s provider. Scoring composition and knockouts are deterministic and provider-free.
Tenant environmentCandidate-facing surfaces (chat apply, status page, infographic JD) render in the tenant’s environment — tenant domain (e.g. careers.vikuna.io), tenant branding, served via a tenant-scoped embed token bound to an origin allowlist. The TA/HM workspace lives on the platform origin.
Usage gateNo JD can be published, and no candidate link goes live, until the tenant passes the onboarding readiness checklist (Section 4).

Section 5

Concept glossary

The nouns of the system. The schema (next deliverable) derives from this table — one concept, one entity.

ConceptDefinition
TenantSubscribing organisation. Isolation boundary for all data below.
Domain packPlatform-level bundle that makes Vara domain-independent: question banks, JD templates, knockout templates, signal-adapter set, vocabulary. Tenants select and override; packs never contain tenant data.
Role familyTenant grouping of similar roles (“Backend Engineering”, “Field Sales”). The unit of calibration and of default thresholds/weights.
JDStructured job definition: role facts, weighted must-haves, nice-to-haves, knockout rules, per-JD threshold (defaulted from role family), renderable as text or infographic. Versioned on edit.
CandidateDurable person entity, tenant-scoped, alive across JDs and years. Owns consent, retention, artifacts, and history. The talent pool is the set of candidates.
ApplicationOne candidate × one JD × one attempt — unique on (candidate, jd, attempt_no). A candidate applies to many roles, and to the same role again after a configurable per-JD cooldown (default 90 days, never a hard block); prior attempts stay visible during screening. A close (rejection) is not an entity — it is the application’s terminal state plus its audit and comms rows.
ArtifactAnything the candidate provided: docx/pdf resume, LinkedIn export, links. Stored raw + parsed + provenance.
Score snapshotImmutable result of scoring one application under one scoring-config version: composite, per-axis scores, evidence citations, flags. Re-scoring creates a new snapshot; never overwrites.
Scoring configVersioned per role family: axis weights, component mappings, threshold default. Changes only via the human-gated calibration flow.
Closing windowTimed state for below-threshold applications. Timer default 3 days (per-JD configurable). Human moves: Rescue, Hold (freeze), Close-now. Expiry ⇒ close + message.
Consistency flagHuman-facing note of a contradiction between sources (resume vs chat vs signals). Never affects the score.
Calibration signalAny human action that teaches: rescue, hold, close-now, HM verdict, hire outcome, audit-sample review.

E2

JD Studio

say it → structured, weighted, ruled, published
V-10P2
Compose by conversation. As a TA pro, I describe the role in my words and Vara structures it — facts, weighted must-haves, nice-to-haves, knockouts, threshold.
Every element Vara sets is visibly editable; nothing is locked behind the conversation.Vara explains any weight or rule on demand, citing evidence (“your last two hires…”), and yields to a manual override without argument.
V-11P2
Author knockouts as rules. As a TA pro, I declare knockouts (notice ≤ N, band ≤ X, mandatory credential) knowing they are the only thing that can auto-close.
Knockouts are deterministic expressions over intake answers — no model in the path.Editing a knockout warns about in-flight candidates; rules apply to new applicants only unless explicitly re-run.
V-12P2
Own the threshold. As a TA pro, I see the JD’s threshold defaulted from its role family and set my own line for this JD.
Threshold changes re-route in-flight applications live (map + closing window) and are audit-logged.A live preview shows the split (“closing: N / handover: M”) before I commit.
V-13P2
Publish everywhere from one schema. As a TA pro, I render the same JD as text or infographic and publish the chat-apply link/QR.
Infographic is a render of the JD schema — no second source of truth.Apply link is tenant-branded and live only post-readiness-gate.
V-14P2
Version, don’t mutate. As a TA pro, when I edit a published JD, existing scores stay honest.
Material edits (weights, knockouts, threshold default) create a JD version; existing snapshots keep the version that scored them.Optional re-score is explicit, creates new snapshots, and notifies no candidate automatically.
E3

Candidate intake

a conversation, not a form — any domain
V-20P4
Apply by chatting. As a candidate, I apply through a short conversation whose questions come from the JD itself.
Questions are generated from must-haves and knockouts — JD and evaluation share one schema.Target ≤ 5 minutes; progress (“profile strength”) is visible; resumable if interrupted.
V-21P4
Know the rules up front. As a candidate, I see the band and hard limits before investing time, and I’m told when an answer trips a limit.
Comp band and knockout limits are stated in-chat, not hidden.A tripping answer triggers an honest explanation and an offer to record a negotiable value instead (e.g., notice “90 → negotiable 45”).
V-22P4
Share what shows me best. As a candidate, I upload a resume (docx/pdf), or share LinkedIn export, portfolio, GitHub, publications — whatever fits my field.
Accepted artifact types come from the JD’s domain pack; a resume-only application is first-class and never penalised for absent signals.Every artifact is stored raw with provenance before any parsing.
V-23P4
Control my data. As a candidate, I give informed consent in the conversation and can exercise my rights from the same channel.
Consent (versioned text) recorded before artifacts are processed.“Delete my data” in chat triggers the purge flow; status link shows what is held and until when.
V-24P4
Never a black hole. As a candidate, I am acknowledged immediately and told the decision within the stated SLA, whatever it is.
Ack on email + WhatsApp within minutes of applying; live status page per application.Rejection messages carry feedback themes, not silence; sent only after the closing window or a human/rule decision.
E4

Ingestion

the ETL spine, generalised — see Flow D3
V-30P5
One spine, many adapters. As the platform, ingestion reuses the existing ETL pipeline shape — source → staging → normalise → validate → commit — with per-source adapters.
docx, pdf, LinkedIn-export, GitHub-API, URL/blog and CSV are all adapters on the same spine; adding an adapter touches no downstream stage.Document parsing is deterministic (libraries, not LLM).
V-31P5
Extract with evidence. As the platform, one fenced LLM stage maps parsed text to the candidate schema.
Every extracted field carries an evidence span (source + location) and a confidence value.Low-confidence fields are marked, never silently guessed; schema validation rejects malformed extractions back through retry.
V-32P5
Recognise returning people. As the platform, entity resolution matches a new application to an existing pool candidate.
High-confidence matches merge automatically with history preserved; ambiguous matches queue for TA confirmation.A merge never deletes either side’s artifacts or audit rows.
E5

Screening & scoring

rules first, then ranked evidence
V-40P2
Rules run first. As a TA pro, knockouts evaluate before any scoring, and a knockout close records exactly which rule and which answer.
Knockout closes are the only human-free closes; message uses the polite template with the rule’s public phrasing.“Why was this person closed?” is answerable from stored data in one click.
V-41P2
Score with citations. As a TA pro, every score is a snapshot: composite, three axes (capability / availability / expectations), each component citing its evidence.
Snapshots are immutable and stamped with scoring-config version; re-scoring appends, never overwrites.The metering event writes at snapshot time.
V-42P2
Flag contradictions, don’t punish them. As a TA pro, I see consistency flags (resume vs chat vs signals) as questions to ask, with zero score impact.
Flags render on map, profile and handover card; each states both sources verbatim.Flag logic never feeds the composite.
V-43P2
See the whole field, move the line. As a TA pro, the probability map shows every scored applicant by fitment; dragging the threshold re-routes live.
Counters, zones and the closing window respond in real time; the drag is audit-logged as a policy change.Every dot opens the full profile.
V-44P2
Stay usable at 250. As a TA pro with a heavy JD, the UX compresses my attention instead of my data.
Map stays readable and interactive at 250+ applicants (dense mode).Lists are ranked with picks on top, searchable, and bulk-actionable; nothing requires reading top-to-bottom.
E6

Closing window

nothing closes instantly
V-50P2
Timer, not trapdoor. As a TA pro, every below-line application enters a visible window (default 3 days, per-JD configurable) before any message fires.
Ack goes out immediately; the decision message only on expiry or explicit close.Window length is a JD field; changing it affects new entries only.
V-51P2
Vara’s picks. As a TA pro, Vara surfaces the below-line candidates worth a second look, with the reason the composite hid them.
Each pick states its strongest axis and the dragging factor in one line.Picks are recommendations — the full ranked window list is always one glance below.
V-52P2
Three moves, all mine. As a TA pro, I can Rescue (→ handover), Hold (freeze the timer), or Close-now on any window candidate — singly or in bulk.
Holds freeze indefinitely and survive threshold changes; resume restarts the remaining time.Every move is logged as a calibration signal with who/when.
V-53P2
Expiry closes honestly. As a TA pro, an untouched expiry closes the application with the reason recorded and the feedback-themed message sent.
Close reason = below threshold T under config vN, stored verbatim.The candidate lands in the talent pool with future-fit tags unless consent was declined.

Section 9

Calibration loop

Vara v1 is self-calibrating with a human gate — deliberately not self-learning. Learning happens at role-family level (per-JD volumes are statistically nothing), in monthly batches, through proposals a named human approves.

→

Section 10

Domain independence

Vara hires for any function, not just technology. The invariant core is domain-blind; everything domain-specific lives in a domain pack — the same pack pattern already adopted for vertical variance in VaNi.

Invariant core (never forks per domain)

Three axesCapability fit · Availability · Expectations — universal across functions; only the components inside them change.
LifecycleThe Flow D2 state machine, closing window, handover — identical everywhere.

Cross-cutting patterns (apply mechanically)

PatternRule
RLS — every tenant-scoped tableALTER TABLE t ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;
CREATE POLICY tenant_isolation ON t USING (tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid);
Applies to all vani_ tenant-scoped and all vara_ tables. Platform artifacts (vani_agent, vani_agent_role, vani_domain_pack) carry no tenant_id and no policy.
Append-only enforcementvara_score_snapshot, vara_scoring_config, vara_jd_version, vara_consent, vara_artifact, vara_calibration_signal, vani_audit_log, vani_comms_log, vani_metering_event: REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE from the app role + a BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE trigger raising an exception. Purge (DPDP) is the single sanctioned exception, implemented as a SECURITY DEFINER function that deletes vara_candidate_pii and redacts audit payloads.
State transitionsvara_application.state changes only through a transition function that validates the Flow D2 edges, writes vani_audit_log in the same transaction, and rejects any transition where actor_type would be “model”.
Keys & indexesFKs as noted; hot paths indexed: application (tenant_id, jd_id, state), (tenant_id, candidate_id) · snapshot (application_id, created_at desc) · signal (tenant_id, family_id, at) · comms_log (ref_entity, ref_id).
