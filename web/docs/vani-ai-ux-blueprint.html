VaNi’s summary

I assessed your responses across the ten failure modes of enterprise AI. Your initiatives did not fail for technical reasons — the model-side answers are among your healthiest. The pattern I found is organizational: no business owner carried the outcome (78% exposure), the pilot had no path to production (64%), and costs were never modeled at unit level (57%). Each of these is known, fixable, and — importantly — your sunk investment is partly recoverable. My recommended first move is below.

Your top three exposures

F4 · Ownership vacuum78%

Initiatives were owned by IT while business sponsors disengaged after kickoff. Nobody was accountable for the outcome, so when the sponsor moved on, the initiative stalled without a decision ever being taken.

Fix direction: a named business owner with outcome KPIs — the operating-model change a fractional Chief AI Officer installs.

F3 · POC-to-production gap64%

Your pilots impressed and then never shipped. The answers indicate no production architecture, integration or security path was planned at pilot stage — the classic “demo that was never deployable.”

Fix direction: production-first rebuild of the strongest pilot; much of the sunk value is salvageable.

F6 · Economics blowout57%

ROI was estimated at a high level, never at unit level. Costs surprised the team mid-build, which eroded sponsor confidence at exactly the moment ownership was already weak.

Fix direction: unit-economics gate before the next build is funded.
